# COMPACT21_LEARNER_SWAP_V1 — independent evidence audit

## Review scope and causal question

This is a frozen replacement of the Compact21 learner, with BASE (canonical XGB), RIDGE (median imputation, StandardScaler, alpha 30), and LGBM_LAMBDARANK (the existing Phase B parameters). It is not parameter selection from CAGR. All other canonical model components, strategy rules and negative feedback must remain unchanged. Single-thread execution and dependency versions are common controlled execution settings.

The experiment asks whether a regularized learner or a different ranker is less sensitive to three observed raw-data perturbations while preserving useful selection. It does not identify every cause of XGB instability, estimate a population variance, or establish future trading performance.

The prior Phase B Ridge result is promising but limited: only Repeat1–Repeat3 common inference was compared and the three training cohorts were intersected. The new native-cohort, all-three-pair protocol is intentionally more demanding. Ridge pointwise fitting uses continuous percentile labels, whereas BASE and LambdaRank use the canonical rounded integer relevance labels. Therefore RIDGE is a frozen learner-plus-objective comparison, not a pure attribution of learner differences holding the target representation fixed.

## Frozen gates

For the monthly diagnostic, require at least 25% reduction in common-input rank MAD, common Top1 disagreement and stability Spearman no worse than BASE, native Top1 disagreement no worse, NDCG@5 at least 95% of BASE, selected-Top1 realized percentile at least 95% of BASE, strict maturity, and exact independent same-input refit predictions. All gates are conjunctive. Constant or weak models cannot advance on apparent stability alone.

All nine complete raw replays (BASE, RIDGE and LGBM_LAMBDARANK on each of three snapshots) are unconditional diagnostics, frozen before new outcomes. For the full diagnostic gate, require CAGR span no more than 75% of new BASE, daily Top1 disagreement no worse, mean CAGR at least 95% of BASE, mean MaxDD no more than two percentage points worse, annual turnover no more than 110% of BASE, and all causal/source/preservation evidence. Joint research-candidate qualification requires both the monthly gate and the complete-strategy gate; neither combination authorizes production adoption; an unconditional replay cannot rescue a failed monthly gate. A failed gate remains recorded; no threshold or hyperparameter is altered after viewing the outcome. These engineering criteria do not imply statistical significance.

The three snapshots are sequential observations of the same historical market, not independent investment trials. Pairwise comparisons share snapshots, annual folds reuse training history, and overlapping returns are dependent. Equal annual/pair means are a declared descriptive aggregation, not an effective sample size of 30 independent observations. Original149 is heavily inspected development evidence; independent vintage/universe or forward validation remains necessary before production adoption.

## Required controls and retained evidence

- Retain each snapshot's native eligible training rows. Only align evaluation keys for the shared-input diagnostic. Validate unique ordered `(signal_date, ticker)` keys and nonempty query groups.
- Enforce both signal date and actual 21-session exit date strictly before the annual January 1 cutoff, with nonmissing finite labels. Imputation and standardization fit only on those rows. Future-row mutation must leave historical eligible fit data, learned state and predictions unchanged.
- Keep monthly diagnostic decision disagreement distinct from daily complete-strategy leader disagreement. Evaluate predictive quality on the same declared mature native rowsets across candidates and BASE.
- Reject nonfinite predictions, missing years/pairs/quality cells, unverified source identities, lost dates and silent inner joins. Record native coverage and feature missingness: median-imputed all-missing or nearconstant designs can make apparently stable models uninformative.
- Preserve canonical Compact63, Tail, macro and ensemble inputs/predictions for the same raw repeat. Canonical `models.variants` independently percentile-ranks Compact21 and Tail before its 0.7/0.3 blend, so raw score units do not directly change blending weights; tie structure can still change.
- Retain actual MA3 panels and ensemble predictions, with semantic fingerprints over schema, canonical row/key order and values, alongside file hashes. Exact same-repeat semantic equality is required. A pickle-byte mismatch alone is insufficient evidence of value inequality; matching economics alone is insufficient evidence of semantic equality.
- Report serialized-panel byte hashes separately from canonical value fingerprints. Do not erase or retrospectively reclassify the prior line's unresolved MA3 artifacts: its panels were not retained, so a new replay cannot establish equality of those missing prior panels.

## Margin diagnostic

For scores on a consistently defined normalized scale, let epsilon be the maximum cross-snapshot per-item score deviation and let m be the first-to-second margin. The inequality `m > 2 * epsilon` is a sufficient invariant-winner condition on the compared item universe. If measured per pair it describes those observed perturbations; it is not a certificate against future noise. The implementation declares per-query centering and unit RMS for each aligned score vector, an affine-invariant descriptive convention. Its parameters are diagnostics of those inference vectors, never fitted preprocessing or trading confidence estimates. Arbitrary raw-unit comparisons between algorithms or different item coverage are invalid. Margins below the bound do not prove a change is necessary. Every observed swap must fall below this sufficient bound, so a count labelled near-gap swaps is tautological and is not evidence of economic near-parity; continuous margin/epsilon values and flat/tie cases must accompany it. Exact ties and deterministic ticker tie-breaking require explicit reporting.
