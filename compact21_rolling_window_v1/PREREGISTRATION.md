# COMPACT21_ROLLING_WINDOW_V1 — registered before candidate fits

The user hypothesis is that the annual expanding history, which includes signals from 2004 even in the 2026 fit, amplifies repeat-download differences or weighs stale relationships. Test this directly in the unchanged canonical BASE/XGB learner. This experiment follows the four-mask feature ablation (run 37674083527): none of its variants met the joint stability/quality gates. No feature or label treatment from that trial is carried over.

The annual fit years are 2017–2026. The controls retain the original expanding training set with both signal_date and actual exit_date_21 strictly before January 1 of the fit year. Three candidates use exactly 6, 10, or 15 prior calendar years of signal dates, starting January 1 of `fit_year - years`, with the same strict outcome-maturity cutoff. They are named ROLL6, ROLL10 and ROLL15. All three start from the original eligible training cohort; no row can be added, and all 125 original features and continuous target data remain byte-identical for retained rows. The original XGB relevance rounding, 360 rounds, three seeds and other parameters are unchanged. Each snapshot is fitted separately. Native inference retains each snapshot's original year frame and common inference uses the original Repeat2 frame.

Six years retains roughly 70 prior monthly training dates in the earliest fold while materially excluding old history; ten years is an intermediate reduction; fifteen years is a long-memory control and equals expanding until sufficiently late in the backtest. These choices express a predetermined dose comparison, not a search over observed outcomes. The training volume and calendar coverage for each fit must be reported. The change in cohort is the sole intervention; features, labels, cutoffs, evaluation dates and outcomes stay frozen.

All original BASE controls must reproduce retained prediction bytes and complete metadata before any candidate fit. Every annual/vintage candidate is independently refit, with identical native/common prediction bytes and audit. Source SHA, frozen TI panel hashes, numeric profile and candidate training matrix/label/group hashes are retained and independently checked against physical panels. Any control/refit/coverage/maturity mismatch invalidates the result.

Primary question: does rolling training reduce instability **between acquisitions of the same historical data**? Report 114 monthly dates and all pairs 1–2, 1–3, 2–3, native Top1 disagreement and Repeat2-common rank MAD. Then assess 113 mature quality dates, Top1 realized percentile, Precision@5 and NDCG@5, overall and by acquisition. Reuse unchanged BLOCKBAG gates: pilot ≥25% reduction in both stability measures with no worse pair; near repeatability native Top1 disagreement ≤5% per pair and common rank MAD ≤10% BASE per pair; quality conservation overall and each-vintage Top1 percentile ≥95% BASE, overall Precision@5 ≥95% BASE, and paired 3-calendar-month bootstrap one-sided lower95 delta > −5% BASE Top1 percentile. Report uncertainty and secondary diagnostics; no weighted score or post-hoc threshold adjustment. No one-window “winner” is selected unless it passes stability and quality jointly; report all three regardless.

These development snapshots have already been examined in prior experiments. Any apparent superiority needs a genuinely new acquisition batch and future mature labels, then unchanged full-strategy replay including decisions/CAGR/drawdown/turnover. No automatic model adoption, negative-feedback edit, Compact63/Tail/MA3 change or portfolio code change is authorized here. Rolling windows can reduce stale data exposure but can also raise variance because fewer labels are available; this experiment does not assume either outcome.
