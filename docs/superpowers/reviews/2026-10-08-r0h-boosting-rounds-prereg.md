# R0-H — BOOSTING-ROUND COUNT: preregistered prefix diagnostic (NO ETF backtest)
**2026-10-08. Frozen before training new synthetic models.**
**Repo/branch:** `AM1975MA/Test@research/v2-xgb-immutable-checkpoints-20261008`.

## Why this test is new
Earlier R0-E varied maximum leaf count with 120 trees; R0-F fixed 360 rounds while varying `max_leaves` and X/Y perturbations. **No controlled outcome by boosting-round prefix was recorded**. The user's missing question is whether late boosting trees disproportionately magnify small X/y disturbances while adding little ranking skill. Original Compact21 XGB uses `rank:pairwise`, 125 features, `max_depth=4`, 360 rounds **per seed and horizon**, seeds 101, 202, 303, ensemble averages raw seed predictions. This new test holds all structure fixed and varies **only the number of trees used for prediction**.

## One controlled synthetic diagnostic, no parameter hunting
- **Data:** only new deterministic synthetic worlds A seed `20261011` and B seed `20261012`, generated by exactly the analytic functions of R0-F `synthetic_world` (36 training groups × 40 rows = 1440, 12 *independent* eval groups × 40 rows = 480, 125 features, 0–100 ranking labels from within-group latent outcomes). Unlike R0-F 20261009/20261010, **these are new artificial draws**, not a rerun of previously reported results.
- **Train variants (same exact groups/eval inputs):** `original`, `X_only` (feature jitter SD `2.5e-6` on standardized features, fixed RNG seed = worldseed xor `0xABCDEF`, original labels), `Y_only` (one grade label changed to strictly invert at least one within-query pair, no feature alteration). No `X_and_Y` or capacity changes (already studied in R0-F).
- **XGB**: original baseline `max_leaves=0` (depth-based cap), `max_depth=4`, `eta=0.035`, `subsample=.85`, `colsample_bytree=.8`, `min_child_weight=8`, `reg_lambda=8`, `reg_alpha=.1`, `tree_method=hist`, `rank:pairwise`, XGBoost 3.1.3, seeds `101,202,303`, nthread=1, 360 rounds, no early stopping and no validation tuning. Train **2 worlds ×3 scenarios×3 seeds=18** boosters at 360, independently and without reusing fitted trees across X/Y changed training inputs.
- **Prefix counts fixed BEFORE inspecting outcomes:** `[60,120,180,240,300,360]`; evaluate `booster.predict(dtest,iteration_range=(0,rounds))` on fixed 12×40 synthetic candidates, using source-style `QuantileDMatrix(Xte,ref=dtrain)`. The prefixes of a standard `gbtree` model give the result of stopping a training trajectory at the respective rounds without rerunning all fits for each prefix. Check using **one additional 60-round canonical-fit sanity** on the same new synthetic world/seed and compare predictions bitwise or `maxAbs<=1e-6` with the 60-round prefix; report exact deviation. No 60/120/etc extra optimization fits.
- **Ensemble:** average the three raw `Booster.predict` arrays as original source-only worker does; separately record seed-101 single-model stability to avoid attributing ensemble robustness to tree count.
- **Metrics for each world and prefix:** baseline Top1 hit among latent realized synthetic best five (of 40) on 12 evaluation groups, baseline NDCG@5 against test-group latent 0–100 ranks, baseline best-decile capture; Top1 agreement baseline vs each X-only/Y-only perturbed ensemble, Top5 Jaccard, mean absolute rank-percentile difference, mean absolute raw-score delta. Independent quality of perturbed predictions also reported.
- **No posthoc optimal prefix:** report all 6 prefixes in both worlds and 3-channels, no numerical threshold chosen from 2017–2026 ETF return, no sorting by a made-up financial CAGR, no additional round thresholds after observing results.
- **Audit:** train groups identical per world, Y flips >=1 actual ranked pair; X-only y bit-identical and Y-only X bit-identical; 360 true boosted rounds each fitted booster; predict prefix matches a separately trained 60-round sanity reference; no effects on original GitHub vendor or ETF files; logs/code/test report on branch `Test` only.
- **Interpretation:** even if 120/180 trees is more stable *and* better on one artificial world, only synthetic mechanism support. The fixed 360 baseline may or may not be optimal on real ETF data, and a real financial challenger would require prospective as-of point-in-time samples with full V2 portfolio run, not burned Original149, and user-approved numeric downside gates. If reduced round count causes loss of synthetic top-tail capture, preserve that as a warning, not a reason to choose another count.
